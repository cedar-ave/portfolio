# Adaptive-Binding Data Warehouse

<Subtitle>It all starts with a data warehouse.</Subtitle>

Most large healthcare organizations have hundreds of analytics vendors. Without bringing all of their data into a data warehouse, reliable and repeatable reporting and analysis is impossible.

Clearpath's Adaptive-Binding Data Warehouse is a revolutionary architectural model for healthcare analytics. When an organization combines a data warehouse with the power of adaptive binding, it quickly progresses to registries and reporting, population health, and clinical and financial risk modeling.

## Adaptive-binding architecture

Data must undergo massive transformations to fit into an enterprise data model.

### The pitfalls of early binding

Early binding architectures — like traditional enterprise data modeling approaches — force early data bindings into proprietary enterprise data models. Time has proven early data bindings to be inflexible, one-size-fits-all solutions, enforcing a compromised, least-common-denominator warehouse.

### The power of adaptive binding

Clearpath's adaptive-binding architecture avoids those inherent limitations. By delaying data binding until the proper time and context, data retains its original, undiluted value.

## Clearpath's adaptive-binding principles

Data warehouses in the military, manufacturing, and healthcare that have operated by these principles for more than 20 years continue to deliver an unparalleled track record of proven results.

1. Minimize remodeling data in the data warehouse until the analytic use case requires it. Leverage the natural data models of the source systems by reflecting much of the same data modeling in the data warehouse.
2. Delay binding to rules and vocabulary as long as possible, until a clear analytic use case requires it.
3. Earlier binding is appropriate for business rules or vocabularies that change infrequently, or that the organization wants to lock down for consistent analytics.
4. Late binding in the visualization layer is appropriate for what-if scenario analysis.
5. Retain a record of the changes to vocabulary and rule bindings in the data models of the data warehouse. This will provide a self-contained configuration control history that can be invaluable for conducting retrospective analysis that feeds forecasting and predictive analytics.
